# Harness: lessons from breaking our own manipulation programs

Each rule below comes from a program that worked in the nominal scene and failed once the scene changed.

## Before writing code

Observe read-only and answer these for every motion.

1. **Swept volume.** What does each motion, including pulls, twists and retreats, sweep with open fingers, palm (about 20 cm along the closing axis), wrist, forearm and held object? Check it, overhead included, against every `get_obstacles()` box, and pass obstacles to every `move_to`. The planner does not model the gripper or what it holds.
   *Prevents:* hand or carried object hitting clutter and shelves while the planner reports success.
2. **Movable blockers.** Does anything movable stand in that volume, around the grasp, or in the goal? Relocate it first, hand empty, clear of goal and carry path. Lift-test it and remember it as immovable if it did not rise.
   *Prevents:* giving up, or cycling approach angles, when one object blocks every approach.
3. **Goal free and flexible.** Is the goal empty, including a container's inside? Treat it as a region of equivalent poses and pick one at run time from perceived free, reachable space.
   *Prevents:* placing onto an occupant or into a hard-coded spot that clutter has taken.
4. **Gripper vs object.** Does the gripped part fit the opening with margin and stand taller than the fingers' minimum height? Will the grip hold at realistic mass and friction, given its distance from the centre of mass?
   *Prevents:* grasps closing on air, objects too wide to pick, heavy objects pivoting or slipping out.
5. **Reach.** Is every pose (grasp, lift, carry, release, exit) inside a comfortable reach margin? If not, lean the approach away from the base or first move the object toward the workspace centre.
   *Prevents:* reach failures misread as collisions, long detours, badly tracked motions at the limit.

## Constraints

6. **Bounded budget.** Try cheap-to-refuse options first, cap retries per method, never repeat a refused motion unchanged, and check that clearing or reorienting fits the remaining time.
   *Prevents:* spending every attempt on one blocked grasp or clearing step.
7. **Alternatives.** Every check that can reject the default needs alternatives across grasp families and approach directions (yaw, tilt, top, side). Grasp where clutter allows, then reorient in free space.
   *Prevents:* giving up when the single grasp or place pose is blocked.
8. **Everything from perception.** Take every coordinate, height, size and axis from the measured 6-DoF pose in the object's own frame, relative to its actual support, with one branch per pose class (upright, lying, flipped). Measure progress relative to the manipulated object's base.
   *Prevents:* failures when support, orientation or size differs from a nominal constant.
9. **Non-prehensile fallback.** When no grasp fits where the object lies, push or slide it into free space or pick it any way and regrasp, then look again.
   *Prevents:* abandoning objects against walls, under overhangs or beyond comfortable reach.
10. **Clean release.** Open only as wide as needed, keep fingers out of the released object's path, retreat along the approach axis and lift clear before travelling.
    *Prevents:* knocking the placed object.

## Self-checks without a simulator

11. **Verify effects, not flags.** After every move compare `tcp_pose()` with the target; after a grasp check `gripper_holding()` and that the object rose in a short test lift. Release only at the planned pose. A refused motion triggers an alternative; never skip to the next waypoint or drop what you hold. Re-verify all goal relations at the end.
    *Prevents:* pulls with an empty hand, mid-air releases, false success.
12. **Re-perceive before contact.** Right before each grasp, release, insertion or push, re-detect target, goal and obstacles; `track` briefly to confirm stillness (averaging hides motion) and re-target if anything moved.
    *Prevents:* acting on stale positions of moved objects.
13. **Robust perception.** Average noisy detections, keep moved/arrived tolerances well above noise, normalise image thresholds by a reference in the same image, and treat an empty detection as failure, never as a nominal value.
    *Prevents:* needless replans and silent perception failures under new lighting or occlusion.
14. **Incremental contact.** Move through contact in short straight steps from the measured pose, slowly under resistance, checking after each that the object moved and is still held. Bound every corrective loop.
    *Prevents:* unnoticed slips during pulls and pushes.

## Workflow

1. Observe; answer 1-5 and list every scene assumption.
2. Write the program with checks 11-14 at every step and alternatives (6-9) behind every check.
3. **Review constants and fallbacks (rule 15).** Which numbers are world constants rather than measurements, which branch covers only one instance, which size limit was considered in one direction only?
   *Prevents:* fixes that cover one blocker, height or size and break on the next.
4. Submit. Start each later attempt from fresh perception.
